## 2. What WorkChamp is

WorkChamp is an AI enablement business for marketers. It sells two things:

1. **AI workflows and operations.** Custom AI built into the tools a marketing team already runs. Agents, prompt libraries, governance, stack integration, and the training that makes people actually use it. At the top end, WorkChamp also designs and builds custom AI apps and software when the tool a client needs does not exist. This is the deepest tier of the same service, not a separate offer.
2. **SEO and AI search.** A full organic search program, plus the newer work of getting the brand cited inside AI answers.

It is run by Dave Thomas, who has spent over a decade running marketing and SEO inside agencies and for brands. That is the credibility the copy leans on. WorkChamp is the parent brand of a product suite that includes PipeChamp, a HubSpot pipeline analytics tool. The suite is background for you only and must not appear in the copy. See section 8.

**Current stage:** early. No published case studies, no named clients, no public pricing. See section 12, because this shapes what you can and cannot write.

## 3. The shift we are positioning against

Marketers are being hit by two changes at once, and most vendors only sell against one of them.

**How the work gets done.** Everyone on the team is using AI, privately and inconsistently. Output quality swings. Nobody owns the process. The tools got bought and the process never arrived.

**How buyers find you.** Search is not returning clicks the way it did. Two thirds of US Google searches now end without a click. When an AI Overview appears, click-through on results below it falls by close to sixty percent. At the same time, more people are starting their research inside ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, where the question is whether you are cited, not whether you rank ninth.

WorkChamp's argument is that these are one problem with one foundation. The content system that speeds a team up is the same system that earns citations. The structured data that makes a site machine readable is the same data an internal agent reads. Buying the two from separate vendors means paying to build one foundation twice.

That argument is the spine of the site. Every page should be traceable back to it.

## 4. Who you are writing for

Two buyers. Both are marketers. Do not write for a general business audience and do not explain what SEO is.

### A. The agency owner or partner

Runs a 10 to 50 person agency. Personally responsible for margin, delivery and whether the team can absorb another client. Their team is already using AI in an uncontrolled way and it is starting to show in the work. Clients are asking about AI search and they do not have an answer yet, so they either lose the conversation or white-label something they cannot stand behind.

- **Believes:** they are the ones who should be selling AI services, not buying them.
- **Fears:** looking behind in front of a client. Paying for a system the team quietly ignores. Losing a senior and losing what that person knew.
- **Buys when:** the thing is concrete, fast, and it either protects margin or lets them sell something new.
- **Allergic to:** being lectured about AI by someone who has never run delivery.

### B. The in-house marketing leader

Head of marketing, marketing director or VP. Small team, big remit. Accountable for pipeline. Watching organic traffic decline and unable to explain it cleanly to a CEO or a board. Team is stretched and most of the week goes to reporting, coordination and admin rather than strategy.

- **Believes:** AI should already be saving them time and mostly is not.
- **Fears:** picking the wrong tool, a governance or brand-safety incident, spending budget on something with no measurable result.
- **Buys when:** there is a defined scope with a start and an end, and a number they can report upward.
- **Allergic to:** vague transformation language, and anything that sounds like it needs a change-management programme.

**What they share:** both are competent marketers who are behind on this one thing and do not enjoy admitting it. Write to someone smart who is short on time, not to someone who needs convincing that AI matters.

## 6. Messaging pillars

Four. Each page should hit at least three. Nothing in the copy should sit outside them.

**1. Both halves, one foundation.**
Operations and search are the same problem. This is the reason WorkChamp exists and the hardest thing to explain. It needs your best writing.

**2. Built around how you already work.**
Custom, wired into the existing stack, shaped to the team's real process. The range runs from a prompt library to a purpose-built application, and the client owns whatever gets built. This is what separates WorkChamp from consultants who can only advise.

**3. People first, and it survives our exit.**
AI is leverage for good marketers. The team is trained, the system is documented, the client is not renting attention forever. AI drafts, named humans send.

**4. Honest measurement.**
Baselines set on day one, sources shown, no vanity metrics, and no promises about things nobody controls. This is a differentiator in a category full of guarantees that cannot be kept.

## 7. Voice

**Sounds like:** a senior marketer explaining something plainly to a peer over coffee. Direct, specific, a little dry. Confident without swagger. Comfortable saying what will not work.

**Hard rules, no exceptions:**

- **No em dashes anywhere.** Use a comma, a full stop, or restructure the sentence. This is a firm house rule.
- **No emojis.**
- **No "it's not X, it's Y" construction**, in any of its forms. Examples that were in an earlier draft and have since been cut: "Not a tool purchase. A working system." and "Not to replace your people, but to help them." Say what the thing is. Do not define it by what it is not.
- **No agency speak.** Banned: leverage as a verb, unlock, supercharge, seamless, holistic, synergy, journey, solutions, empower, robust, cutting-edge, game-changing, revolutionise, at scale, best-in-class, transform your business.
- **No AI hype.** Banned: the future of work, AI-powered, harness the power, next-generation, paradigm shift, 10x. WorkChamp sells calm competence, and the category is loud enough already.
- **No fake urgency.** No "don't get left behind", no countdowns, no fear-of-missing-out framing.
- **Second person.** "Your team", not "companies" or "organisations".
- **Contractions are fine.** Formal-but-warm, not stiff.
- **Canadian English spelling.** Optimise stays optimize where it is a search term people type, but colour, behaviour, licence as a noun.
- **Sentence case for headlines**, not title case.
- **Capitalise Trustee** if a Licensed Insolvency Trustee is ever referenced. Unlikely here, but it is a standing house rule.

**Good:** "You bought the tools. You never got the process."
**Bad:** "Unlock the transformative power of AI to supercharge your marketing operations."

**Rhythm note:** the design puts a lot of short cards next to each other. Vary sentence length deliberately so six cards in a row do not read like a spreadsheet.

### Claims that are not permitted

- Any guarantee of a ranking, a ChatGPT mention, a citation, or a traffic number.
- Any specific time saving, percentage improvement, or revenue figure. There is no client data behind those yet. "3 days to 4 hours" currently appears in a homepage graphic as an illustration and needs to either come out or be clearly labelled as an example.
- Client names, logos, testimonials or case studies. None exist yet.
- Team size or "we" implying a large team. WorkChamp is small and the copy should be honest about that. "Senior people only" is a stronger line than implying a bench.
- Years of experience beyond "more than a decade" for Dave personally.
- WorkChamp's own software products. Dave has shipped SaaS, but the decision is to keep those brands out of the services story. Do not reference them, even unnamed.
- Named examples of apps built for clients, until section 12 item 7 is supplied.

### The credibility problem, stated plainly

There is no proof on this site. No clients, no numbers, no logos. That means the copy carries the entire burden. The way to do that is specificity, not superlatives. A line that names the exact tool, the exact task, the exact failure mode reads as experience. A line that claims a result reads as marketing. Write the first kind.

The agencies page currently has a scrolling marquee of invented agency names presented as clients. That is coming out. Do not write around it.

## 12. What we owe you before you start

This brief cannot be executed well without the following from Dave. Flag it if any are still missing when you begin.

1. **The consultation promise.** The copy says a reader leaves with "three concrete opportunities". Confirm that is deliverable every time, or change the promise.
2. **Pricing posture.** Is a sprint priced publicly, given as a range, or held for the call? This affects how hard the pages have to work.
3. **The AI Readiness Assessment.** Either it exists and gets a page, or the secondary CTA comes off both pages. Do not write around a dead link.
4. **The illustrative time-saving figure** in the homepage hero graphic. Real example, labelled hypothetical, or removed.
5. **Three specific stories,** even unnamed and unpublishable as case studies. A specific thing that happened to a real team is what separates this copy from every competitor's. Anonymised detail is enough.
6. **One sentence from Dave on why he started this,** in his own words, unedited. It may become the About line, and it will tell you more about the voice than this brief can.
7. **Two or three shapes of custom app already built,** even anonymised and without results. The custom apps section currently describes categories of work. One real example, described concretely, would be worth more than the whole section.
8. **Where the app building stops.** Is there a size of build WorkChamp turns down, and does it hand off or subcontract anything? The page implies full delivery. Confirm that is true before it goes live.
